Case study 03 · Workflow system & payments onboarding

MFS Branch Wallet Registration Portal

Every branch office collects and disburses by mobile money, so each one needs a merchant wallet with bKash, Nagad or Upay. Getting one used to mean an email thread, an Excel sheet and two to four weeks of waiting, and a single typo against the cheque meant the provider rejected the whole application. This portal turns that into a bilingual maker-checker workflow: the branch submits, the region verifies, head office approves, and the provider pack is generated on letterhead. A number already registered is caught before it ever reaches the provider.

The problem

Mobile financial services are how a microfinance branch in Bangladesh moves money today: collections come in and disbursements go out through bKash, Nagad and Upay. Each branch office therefore needs its own merchant wallet, registered with the provider against the branch's bank account.

The registration itself is simple. The process around it was not. A branch emailed its details to the region, the region copied them into a spreadsheet, head office consolidated the spreadsheets and sent them to the provider, and the provider sent back rejections when a name on the form did not match the name on the cheque. Two to four weeks per branch was normal, and a wallet that was already registered under a different spelling was discovered only when the provider said so.

The rule Nothing reaches a provider until a human at the region has verified it against the cheque image, a second human at head office has approved it, and the mobile number has been checked against everything already registered. Checks before it registers.

What I built

The detail I would defend in an interview

The portal was live for 400+ branch offices one week after the requirement was agreed.

The workflow was already known in detail because I coordinate the mobile-money partnerships myself: I am the single point of contact with bKash, Nagad and Upay, I handle the issue resolution on registrations and account changes, and I had seen every way the email process failed. That let the first version ship with the real state machine, the real reason codes and the real provider layouts rather than approximations, and seven generated user manuals in Bangla and English went out with it.

Two properties were fixed from the start. The cheque image is mandatory, because the name on it is what the provider will check. And the portal generates the provider pack instead of letting anyone type it, because re-typing is where the rejections came from.

A hand-off, not a hand-over The portal prepares and controls the submission. Registration with the provider, and the relationship with the provider, stay with people. The system makes sure what they send is right the first time.

Two more decisions

  • Bilingual by design. Branch users work in Bangla; head office and the providers work in English. Every label, reason code and manual exists in both, generated from one source, so the two audiences never see different rules.
  • Branch data stays inside the organisation. The portal runs on the organisation's own machine behind an authenticated tunnel; no provider or third party can see the register.

Outcome

Live from 21 June 2026 with 750+ branch users. 194 registrations from branches across the network, 57 provider-ready batches, and a median of 0.8 days from submission to regional verification, against two to four weeks by email. Thirteen duplicate numbers stopped before they cost a rejection. The same portal now carries the account-change requests that used to travel by letter.
