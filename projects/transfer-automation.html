Case study 07 · Governance & compliance

Transfer Eligibility & PAR-Migration Governance

Branch managers submit spreadsheets asking to move members between programmes, branches, samities, clusters and field officers. Most requests are ordinary. Some are not: moving an overdue member into a colleague's portfolio makes the delinquency disappear from the original officer's numbers. This tool checks every member's real loan position before a transfer is approved, and records who asked, so the pattern becomes visible even when each single request looks reasonable.

The problem

This tool does two jobs, and the second is the reason it exists.

The operational job is the obvious one. A zone submits a workbook asking to move several hundred members, and somebody has to open several hundred member profiles by hand to find out which of them are carrying an outstanding loan. It takes days and it is the kind of work that gets skipped when the deadline is close.

The governance job is the one nobody asks for. Portfolio at risk is measured per officer. If an overdue member is moved out of one officer's portfolio and into a colleague's, the first officer's delinquency figure improves without a single taka being repaid. The industry term is PAR migration, or portfolio cosmetics. Every individual request looks defensible. The pattern only appears when you can see who has been asking, for whom, and how often, across months and zones.

The rule A member is Not Eligible if they hold an active primary loan with an outstanding balance above zero. Otherwise Eligible. If the profile or the loan table could not be read reliably, the answer is Manual Review — never a yes.

What I built

The detail I would defend in an interview

No language model takes part in an eligibility or fraud decision.

I build with language models elsewhere in this portfolio, including a pipeline of 71 of them. Here I deliberately kept them out of the decision path. A compliance tool has to give the same answer twice and be able to say exactly why. A probabilistic component in that path would mean an officer could be flagged by something no one can reproduce, and a flagged officer deserves a rule they can read.

Two properties follow from the same reasoning. The tool fails loud: a failure to read a page never becomes a quiet "Eligible", it becomes Manual Review. And every decision stores the version of the rule that produced it, so a decision made in June can still be explained after the rule changes in September.

A pre-check, not an approval The tool never approves a transfer. It tells a human what the loan system actually says and records that it was asked. Business sign-off stays where it was.

Two more decisions

  • Evidence, not just verdicts. Each member check keeps the page text and a screenshot. When someone disputes a result months later, the answer is not my parser's opinion but what the system displayed at the time.
  • Member data never leaves the machine. The governance database, the uploaded requests, the generated results and the per-member snapshots are all excluded from version control. Clone the repository and you get the code and the configuration templates, not anyone's loan balance.

Outcome

A manual check that nobody had time to do properly now runs as a batch with evidence attached, and a category of quiet number-moving that was previously invisible has a permanent record. The tool is a working prototype, exercised on test data; the remaining work before a first production run is listed in its handover brief rather than implied.
